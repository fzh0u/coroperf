Issue findings

Local observation
Two branches created at different positions returned 6, 7, 8 and 9 on 3.13.1 and the fix parent. The fixed commit, 3.13.2 and the synchronous itertools reference returned 1, 4, 2 and 5. Three of ten file-backed controls lost records on the old builds.
What CoroPerf showed
On the old build, advancing either child entered tee_peer twice and fetched a new source value. The child had its own buffer and wrapped its parent. On the fixed build, each child inherited the buffer position at its fork, and all four reads were served without another source read.
Scope and limits
The report runs on a single top-level async execution. The evidence identifies iterators and their buffers. All eleven selected upstream tests passed on all four builds; the original reproduction and independent file controls distinguish the faulty behavior.
