Issue findings

Local observation
The v0.29.0 CLI and checked main send three of four requests with one client and two active conversations. Two clients with four three-turn conversations send ten of twelve. Both runs exit with code zero. The loopback POSTs and the exported statistics agree on every conversation and turn. PR #57181 restores all expected turns and preserves the deliberate two-request limit.
What CoroPerf showed
The two response callbacks have identical physical frames, as do the two client exit stacks. Records 2 and 6 attach to the exact sending Tasks through their aiohttp ResponseHandler objects. Records 3 and 7 are those same Tasks at exit. The child PIDs and client indices link their creation records 1 and 5 to the driver launches 0 and 4. The original queue and conversation objects identify one pending third user request at each exit, after the input sentinel and with zero failed requests.
Scope and limits
The experiment runs the entire benchmark CLI, multiprocessing queues, aiohttp client and statistics export on Linux with a local tokenizer and fixed loopback SSE. The traced workers use Python's spawn start method, and the driver's frames are absent from the raw child stacks. Model inference and throughput are unmeasured. The issue and PR #57181 remain open. The separate queue-collection PR #69282 still sends three of four in the minimal case by itself.
