Issue findings

Local observation
With both queries fixed to the report’s November clock, InfluxDB 2.7.3 returned October 13 at 02:49 UTC for a relative month and October 12 at 12:19 UTC for date.sub in Berlin. That moved one boundary reading outside the range and changed the fixture’s spread from 30 to 20. Across five dates and two time zones, nine of ten monthly spreads differed on a counter derived from recorded household electricity use.
What CoroPerf showed
The controller created a query worker at controller.go line 229. On that worker, values.Duration.Duration converted one month to 2,629,800 seconds. ToQueryTime kept the fixed duration. Time.Time then applied it to the request clock, including during physical read planning. The returned start matched to the nanosecond. In the calendar control, date.addDuration preserved Berlin calendar and timezone rules. A separate Python calculation reconstructed every query boundary and spread from the input readings.
Scope and limits
The original report used ARM64 and private meter readings. The local runs used Linux/amd64, six constructed boundary readings, and a historical counter built from 1,569,894 observed minute readings in the UCI dataset by Georges Hebrail and Alice Berard. Missing readings contributed no energy to this counter. Additive source probes recorded operands in two separate query processes. The official binary and unmodified source build independently served the same results.
