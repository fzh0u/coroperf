Issue findings

Local observation
With buffered(2).for_each, both handshakes start. The server sends the second reply when work on the first result begins. The second handshake expires at 10 ms and polls again at 20 ms, after that work completes. These are readings from the paused Tokio clock. The stock Rust backtraces for both handshake resumes are identical, and all three callees share one Tokio task ID. The backtraces omit both launchers.
What CoroPerf showed
The actual pinned Future poll intervals connect the late handshake observation (23) to its creation (1) at launch_inventory. The first result object is observed at emission (10) and at creation of the consuming work Future (13). The displayed work stack connects 13 to its active poll sample (15), and the matching result object connects it back to launch_profile (0). The measured interval contains no poll of the delayed handshake.
Scope and limits
The replay uses in-process oneshot channels, futures-util 0.3.34, and Tokio 1.54.1 on one thread with a paused clock. In the tested application workaround, a bounded result queue lets the second handshake acknowledge at 0 ms. Its expensive work still starts at 20 ms after the first work finishes. Both sides run under one Tokio task ID. Two reruns verify 64 caller attributions for this layout while changing launch order and role assignment. The upstream issue remains open.
