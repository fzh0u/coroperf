Issue findings

Local observation
On CPython 3.10.3, release marks the already-queued task's Future ready. The holder gets the next permit and the earlier waiter stays blocked. The earlier waiter gets the next permit on 3.10.4 and 3.14.3 in this replay. After dispatch, the Task stacks, creation tracebacks, and coroutine origins omit both original launchers. The 3.14.3 call graph shows the current wait sites.
What CoroPerf showed
CoroPerf records the actual Future woken by release and the Task that receives the next permit. The displayed grant stack joins the acquisition to its Task creation, then follows the matching dispatch Handle back to launch_profile. The waiting stack connects to launch_inventory through its own Handle. Withholding the Handle edges leaves both Task creation stacks at the shared dispatch site.
Scope and limits
The two traces show a bounded two-task replay on Linux and CPython 3.10.3. Matched comparisons also ran on 3.10.4 and 3.14.3. The experiment uses one permit and lets both tasks drain through normal completion.
