Issue findings

Local observation
Two producer Tasks raise distinct ValueErrors with the same message and stack. With Nuitka 4.2.2, injecting each stored exception into a fresh compiled async generator drops make_exception from the traceback. Nuitka 4.3 and interpreted CPython preserve that frame. The receiver backtraces, Task creation stacks, and coroutine origins are identical within the tested builds and omit both original launchers.
What CoroPerf showed
CoroPerf records make_exception before handoff (3 and 4), under the actual producer Tasks started by launch_profile (1) and launch_inventory (2). The same exception object is passed to the corresponding receiver at Task creation (7 and 8) and caught after injection (9 and 10). The displayed chains 1 → 7 → 9 and 2 → 8 → 10 recover the distinct origin callers. The first-raise observations show the frame that disappears from the affected receiver tracebacks.
Scope and limits
This Linux x86-64 replay extends the reported athrow example using two producer Tasks and two receiver Tasks. It tests Nuitka 4.2.2 and 4.3 with CPython 3.11.15, plus interpreted CPython. Both batches verify 48 receiver joins across independent changes in launch and handoff order. Withholding the object handoff leaves the receiver traceback linked to only the common dispatcher. Capture uses scoped Python application probes and the CoroPerf asyncio agent.
