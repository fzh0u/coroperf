Issue findings

Local observation
The original program returned 10 for January through May and changed its time coordinate from seconds to microseconds. Using 144 monthly passenger counts, we reopened two Arrow files covering six years each. Strict concatenation failed for second, millisecond and nanosecond input. The merged repair preserved all four units and joined all twelve annual totals.
What CoroPerf showed
The stacks follow DataArray.resample into SeasonResampler.factorize and both calls to pandas DatetimeIndex.__new__. The old constructor received plain Python datetime labels built from year, month and day. Its returned index used microseconds even when the source used seconds. The fixed constructor received NumPy datetime64 labels carrying the source unit. We independently checked all output dates and counts, including the 1949 January–May total of 612 thousand passengers.
Scope and limits
The original report used macOS arm64. These Linux x86-64 runs matched its xarray commit, Python, pandas and NumPy versions. The two-file Arrow operation is a downstream test added for this study. A safe explicit cast also restored the twelve annual records in these data. The captured resampling path is synchronous.
