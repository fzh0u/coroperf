Issue findings

Local observation
On Polars 1.30.0 the last three positive values in the posted array had an exact mean of 0.000058577603340381756, yet the centered rolling mean returned −0.000004144294508 in both runs. In 39 of 51 windows the mean fell outside the input range. Release 1.31.0 kept every posted mean within one Float32 step of its correctly rounded value. On a separate 587-event USGS catalog workload, the old release exceeded one step in 576 windows. At event usp000hw42 it returned 1,603,985.75 for an exact 1,484,142.3671875. The fixed release returned 1,484,142.375. Both releases correctly rounded the ordinary mean of [1, 1, 2] to 1.3333333730697632.
What CoroPerf showed
GDB followed the expression evaluator through rolling_mean and rolling_agg into the released Rust kernel. As the input 92439.234375 left the reporter’s window, 1.30.0 executed the scalar Float32 add vaddss and finished the update with a sum of 0.0000428866. Release 1.31.0 executed the Float64 add vaddsd and retained 0.0002310523. Native state and return registers matched every one of the 51 updates in each run. PR 22944 widened the sliding sum and compensation. Initialization still summed the first window in Float32, and the result still used Float32 division.
Scope and limits
The 51 history variants keep the same final three values. All ten starts retaining the earlier peak gave a negative final mean in 1.30.0. Replacing just that peak with either neighbor gave the exact mean. In 1.31.0 all final means were positive, but starts 7 and 8 remained about 67% and 33% low. At start 7 GDB read an initial sum of 92439.234375 and zero compensation for an exact sum of 92439.23449328267. The wider updates inherited that loss. Each batch checked all 129 live sums, corrections and returned means across starts 7, 8 and 9. After the July initialization rework, all 51 results in 1.32.0 matched the wide-initialization model within one Float32 step of the reference. Release 2.1.0 gave the exact final mean in all 51. Each release control was measured twice. The USGS workload derives a relative energy index from catalog magnitudes. The captured stacks are synchronous.
